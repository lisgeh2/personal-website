"Prevention" is a word we heard all the time in our bachelor's, and the real world mostly
ignores it.

I guess that's kind of fair. We don't really have good models for suffering that isn't
immediately, obviously visible, and it's hard to take something seriously when you can't
see it. But no symptoms is far from being exactly the same as real health.

So here's my attempt at a model of the different levels of "no symptoms". The key is the
**match between the treatment and the cause**: does whatever made the symptoms go away
actually touch the reason they were there?

## Health

**Prevention.** The person never even gets sick in the first place. No cause ever exists.

**Health · one change.** One single change, targeted at the root of the suffering, and
the suffering never returns. Quitting a toxic relationship. Finding meaning in your life,
or a community that loves your authentic you. Glasses. Maybe psychedelics.

**Health · learned.** You change your values or lifestyle routines. It's constant action at
first, but it gets easier over time, to the point where you don't notice it anymore. Like
building muscle, or exposure against a fear.

This is the line that matters: **above it, you can stop and it stays gone. Below it, it
comes back.**

## Coping

**Coping · stable.** You change your values or routines, but a degree of effort or side
effects always stays, because you're not touching the source. Physio exercises for the rest
of your life. Constantly changing your thoughts. SSRIs.

**Coping · eroding.** Same idea, but the effort or costs keep increasing, because you have
to put in more than you can sustain. It's a slow erosion, and at some point it slides all
the way down. Masking is the classic example.

## Harm reduction

The symptoms are technically still there, but the worst outcomes are avoided. Installing a
net so people can't kill themselves. That doesn't heal anything, but it's not nothing
either: keeping someone alive and safe can be the bridge up to actual healing.

## Concealment

The symptoms are still there too. What changes is only how visible they are: suppressing
them, or simply not reporting them anymore. From the outside it looks like the burden
dropped. On the inside it keeps growing.

## Full symptomatology

Full diagnostic criteria and full suffering. The person isn't able to cope anymore.

## So what

Current psychiatry treats that last level as illness, and everything else as healing.

But from the outside, prevention, health, coping, harm reduction and concealment can all
look exactly the same: no symptoms. Only the top three are actually health. The others are
ways of carrying the cause around, some of them sustainable, some of them not, and one of
them invisible.

If we only ever measure whether symptoms are there, we can't tell these apart. And we'll
keep being surprised when someone who "was doing fine" suddenly isn't.
